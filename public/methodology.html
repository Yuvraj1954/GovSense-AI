Performance Scoring Methodology

Every Member of Parliament (MP), Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA), and State/UT is assigned a composite performance score on a 0–100 scale. The score uses two equally weighted indicators, each natively on a 0–100 scale — no min-max normalization is applied.

Indicator Weight Description
Wilson Lower Bound (Completion)50%Bayesian confidence-interval lower bound on completion rate (z=1.96). Penalizes entities with few completed works — a conservative completion proxy, not a raw percentage.
Fund Utilization50%Ratio of actual expenditure to sanctioned allocation, as a percentage (0–100).
Formula: raw = (wilson_lower_bound(completed_works, total_works) + fund_utilization_pct) / 2. Small-sample shrinkage pulls scores toward the midpoint: score = (n × raw + K × 50) / (n + K) where K=5 for members, K=10 for states. Entities with zero works receive NO_DATA; members with <5 works and states with <10 works receive INSUFFICIENT_DATA.

Risk Assessment Model

The AI Risk Centre employs a multi-signal risk classification system that flags entities (MPs, MLAs, States) and individual works based on anomaly detection, historical patterns, and threshold analysis.

Risk Levels

CRITICAL
Multiple severe anomalies across cost, duration, and completion metrics. Immediate attention required.
HIGH
Significant anomalies detected in 2+ dimensions. Pattern suggests systematic issues.
MODERATE
Isolated anomalies within acceptable variance. Monitoring recommended.
LOW
No significant anomalies. Within expected performance parameters.

Anomaly Detection Signals

Cost Anomaly — Works with sanctioned cost exceeding 3× the category median, or expenditure significantly deviating from sanctioned amount.
Duration Anomaly — Works exceeding 24 months without completion, or completed in under 30 days (potential rush completion).
Overdue Works — Works with status "ongoing" whose elapsed time exceeds the category's 90th percentile duration.
Flagged Works — Works matching any of the above anomaly criteria, aggregated for entity-level risk scoring.

Peer Cluster Classification

Entities are grouped into peer clusters to enable meaningful performance comparisons. K-Means clustering runs on 5 standardized features (members) or 6 features (states). Optimal k is chosen dynamically from {2, 3, 4, 5, 6} by silhouette score. Cluster labels are generated from centroid values binned against population tertiles — the two most informative dimensions are kept and joined with " / ".

Feature Source Role
financial_efficiencyfund_utilization_pctRaw 0–100, higher = better
implementation_efficiencycompleted_works / total_works × 100Raw percent, higher = better
timelinessavg_sanction_delay_daysRaw days, lower = better
portfolio_healthComposite: 100 − (flagged_rate + overdue + cost_anomaly penalties)Raw 0–100, higher = healthier
portfolio_size_loglog(total_works + 1)Log-scaled scale proxy
active_members_log (states only)log(active_members + 1)State-level only
Preprocessing: Z-score standardization (mean=0, std=1) per feature. Minimum cluster sizes enforced: 5 (members), 3 (states). Clusters below threshold receive "insufficient" label. Bootstrap ARI stability (5 resamples, 85% fraction) recorded for diagnostic purposes.
Label generation: For each centroid feature (excluding portfolio_size_log), values are binned against population tertiles (33.3rd / 66.6th percentiles) into High/Mid/Low (or Fast/Mid/Slow for timeliness). Only the two most informative dimensions are kept in the final label, e.g. "High Financial Efficiency / Fast Timeliness".

ML Algorithms & Statistical Methods

Govsense AI applies machine learning and statistical techniques across the analytics pipeline. Every model is chosen for interpretability and reproducibility — black-box models are avoided in favour of explainable methods.

Clustering & Classification

  • K-Means Clustering

    Groups MPs/MLAs into peer cohorts on 5 features (members) or 6 features (states): financial_efficiency, implementation_efficiency, timeliness, portfolio_health, portfolio_size_log (+ active_members_log for states). Z-score standardized. Optimal k chosen from {2–6} by silhouette score. Cluster labels generated from centroid tertile bins — two most informative dimensions retained (e.g. "High Financial Efficiency / Fast Timeliness").

  • Performance Scoring (Wilson + Bayesian)

    Two-indicator score: 50% Wilson Lower Bound of completion rate (z=1.96) + 50% Fund Utilization. Both natively 0–100. Small-sample Bayesian shrinkage toward midpoint (K=5 members, K=10 states). No min-max normalization — indicators are already on the same scale.

  • Threshold-Based Classification

    Discretizes the 0–100 score into 5 ordinal classes: EXCEPTIONAL (≥85), PERFORMER (≥70), STABLE (≥50), NEEDS_ATTENTION (≥35), UNDERPERFORMER (<35). NO_DATA when n=0; INSUFFICIENT_DATA when n<5 (members) or n<10 (states).

Anomaly Detection

  • Robust Z-Score (MAD-Based) Outlier Detection

    Entity-level anomaly scoring across 12 member-level features (flagged_rate, high_risk_rate, completion_rate, expenditure_utilization, avg_sanction_delay, overdue_rate, cost_anomaly_rate, duration_anomaly_rate, avg_cost_percentile, expenditure_over_sanction_rate, negative_delay_rate, avg_project_age_days). Uses median and Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) with consistency constant 0.6745 — not mean/std. Per-feature z-scores direction-adjusted (only deviations in the anomalous direction contribute). Composite = mean of contributing z-scores. Classification uses dynamic population percentiles: HIGH (>95th), MEDIUM (>80th), NORMAL.

  • Isolation Forest (ML Anomaly Detection)

    sklearn IsolationForest on 8 numerical features per work: sanction_amount, recommended_amount, expenditure_amount, sanction_delay_days, execution_days, project_age_days, cost_percentile, duration_percentile. Contamination=0.05. Scores normalized using [50th percentile, 95th percentile] range to [0, 1]. Labels: NORMAL (<0.33), UNUSUAL (0.33–0.66), HIGHLY_UNUSUAL (≥0.66). Minimum 30 works required. Currently READY and producing per-work anomaly scores in production.

  • Work-Level Risk Flags (Deterministic)

    10 deterministic flags per work (SANCTION_DELAY_VERY_HIGH, LONG_PENDING, LOW_EXPENDITURE, PAYMENT_STAGNATION, COST_ANOMALY, DURATION_ANOMALY, OVER_EXPENDITURE, POST_COMPLETION_PAYMENT, etc.). Risk level from flag count: HIGH (≥3 flags or OVER_EXPENDITURE present), MEDIUM (2 flags), LOW (1 flag), NORMAL (0 flags). Positive signals (SANCTION_MATCHES_RECOMMENDATION, RECENT_PAYMENT_ACTIVITY, etc.) computed as countervailing evidence.

Trend & Time-Series Analysis

  • Entity Anomaly Scoring

    Robust z-score (MAD-based) across 12 features per entity, compared within same member type (MP vs MLA separately). Dynamic percentile-based thresholds (95th/80th) classify anomaly levels. Confidence levels derived from sample size (≥50 → HIGH, ≥20 → MEDIUM, <20 → LOW).

  • Gemini AI — Contextual Narrative Layer

    Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite generates natural-language summaries, highlights, and cautions for citizen-facing dashboards. It is an explanation layer, not a predictive model — evidence is pre-computed deterministically; Gemini rephrases it into accessible language. Prompt version: gemini_analysis_v5. Forbidden terms prevent exposure of implementation details.

Risk Scoring Model

  • Multi-Source Risk Engine

    Combines 2 evidence sources with equal weighting: (1) Isolation Forest 95th-percentile anomaly score (unsupervised ML), (2) robust z-score entity anomaly (statistical). Score = mean of 2 normalized sources × 100. Risk levels determined by population percentile ranking: CRITICAL (≥90th percentile), HIGH (≥70th), MODERATE (≥40th), LOW (<40th).

  • Confidence Assessment

    Based on sample size of works per entity. Members: ≥20 works → HIGH, ≥2 → MEDIUM, <2 → LOW. States: ≥50 works → HIGH, ≥2 → MEDIUM, <2 → LOW. This is a sample-adequacy label, not a statistical confidence interval.

Key Statistical Principles

Reproducibility: All computations are deterministic given the same DB1 snapshot. K-Means uses fixed random_state=42. Isolation Forest uses deterministic contamination threshold.
Explainability: Every score and classification can be traced to input indicators and weights. Cluster labels are human-readable. Gemini outputs include explicit evidence citations.
Conservatism: When data is insufficient (<5 works for members, <10 for states), the system defaults to NO_DATA or INSUFFICIENT_DATA rather than producing potentially misleading scores.

Planned Methods (Not Yet in Production)

The following methods are part of the design roadmap but are not currently implemented in the production pipeline. They are listed here for transparency and completeness.

Rolling Window Aggregation

3-month and 6-month rolling averages for smoothing short-term volatility. Planned for quarter-over-quarter comparison.

Linear Regression Trend Lines

OLS fitted to monthly expenditure time series. Planned for classifying entities as Accelerating / Stable / Decelerating.

Moving Average Convergence

3-month vs 6-month MA crossovers for detecting fund disbursement inflection points.

Statistical Process Control

Control charts for monthly expenditure/completion against rolling baselines. Planned for early-warning alerts.

Data Pipeline Architecture

Govsense AI operates a two-database architecture with a change-triggered full rebuild pipeline. This ensures data integrity while enabling rapid analytical updates.

DB1 — Raw Operational Store

  • 1.Direct ingestion from MoSPI / NIC MPLADS portal
  • 2.Raw work records, member profiles, fund allocations
  • 3.Fingerprint-based change detection (SHA-256)
  • 4.Preserves original data integrity — no transformations

DB2 — Analytical Intelligence Store

  • 1.All derived tables, ML scores, and intelligence layer
  • 2.Performance scores, risk classifications, peer benchmarks
  • 3.AI-generated analysis narratives per entity
  • 4.Cluster labels, anomaly flags, trend indicators

Known Limitations & Disclaimers

  • •Data reflects publicly available MPLADS records from the NIC portal. Govsense AI does not have access to internal government systems.
  • •Performance scores are derived indicators, not official government rankings. They should be interpreted as analytical insights, not verdicts.
  • •Risk flags are probabilistic assessments based on statistical anomaly detection. They do not imply misconduct or wrongdoing.
  • •Data freshness depends on the NIC portal update cycle. There may be a lag between ground reality and available records.
  • •MLA data coverage varies by state. Some states may have incomplete records.
  • •Gemini AI is an explanation layer, not a predictive model. It generates natural-language summaries and highlights from pre-computed deterministic evidence. It does not produce probability scores or classifications.
