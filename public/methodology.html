Performance Scoring Methodology

Every Member of Parliament (MP), Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA), and State/UT is assigned a composite performance score on a 0–100 scale. The score aggregates multiple weighted indicators to produce a single comparable metric.

Indicator Weight Description
Fund Utilization30%Ratio of actual expenditure to sanctioned allocation
Completion Rate30%Percentage of works completed vs total recommended
Sanction Conversion15%Speed and rate of converting recommendations to sanctioned works
Execution Speed15%Average duration from sanction to completion (lower is better)
Anomaly Resistance10%Inverse of detected cost/duration anomalies

Performance Classifications

EXCEPTIONAL
Score ≥ 85
PERFORMER
Score ≥ 70
STABLE
Score ≥ 50
NEEDS ATTENTION
Score ≥ 35
UNDERPERFORMER
Score < 35
NO DATA
Insufficient records

Risk Assessment Model

The AI Risk Centre employs a multi-signal risk classification system that flags entities (MPs, MLAs, States) and individual works based on anomaly detection, historical patterns, and threshold analysis.

Risk Levels

CRITICAL
Multiple severe anomalies across cost, duration, and completion metrics. Immediate attention required.
HIGH
Significant anomalies detected in 2+ dimensions. Pattern suggests systematic issues.
MODERATE
Isolated anomalies within acceptable variance. Monitoring recommended.
LOW
No significant anomalies. Within expected performance parameters.

Anomaly Detection Signals

Cost Anomaly — Works with sanctioned cost exceeding 3× the category median, or expenditure significantly deviating from sanctioned amount.
Duration Anomaly — Works exceeding 24 months without completion, or completed in under 30 days (potential rush completion).
Overdue Works — Works with status "ongoing" whose elapsed time exceeds the category's 90th percentile duration.
Flagged Works — Works matching any of the above anomaly criteria, aggregated for entity-level risk scoring.

Peer Cluster Classification

Entities are grouped into peer clusters to enable meaningful performance comparisons. Clustering uses k-means on standardized metrics (fund utilization, completion rate, total works) to create comparable cohorts.

High Performer — Strong Completion

Above-average completion rates and fund utilization. Consistent execution track record.

Large Scale — High Activity

High volume of works with moderate completion. Scale compensates for partial underperformance.

Moderate Growth — Emerging

Mid-range performance with improving trends. Room for acceleration in execution.

Needs Improvement — Low Activity

Below-average metrics across multiple dimensions. Priority for intervention and support.

ML Algorithms & Statistical Methods

Govsense AI applies machine learning and statistical techniques across the analytics pipeline. Every model is chosen for interpretability and reproducibility — black-box models are avoided in favour of explainable methods.

Clustering & Classification

  • K-Means Clustering

    Groups MPs/MLAs into performance cohorts based on fund utilization, completion rate, sanction conversion, execution speed, and anomaly resistance. Optimal k selected via elbow method + silhouette score. Produces 4 readable cluster labels (High Performer, Large Scale, Moderate Growth, Needs Improvement).

  • Weighted Composite Scoring

    Multi-indicator performance index using domain-expert weights (Fund Utilization 30%, Completion Rate 30%, Sanction Conversion 15%, Execution Speed 15%, Anomaly Resistance 10%). Min-max normalized per indicator before aggregation.

  • Threshold-Based Classification

    Discretizes continuous scores into 5 ordinal classes (EXCEPTIONAL → UNDERPERFORMER) using fixed thresholds. NO_DATA class assigned when fewer than 3 works exist for the entity.

Anomaly Detection

  • Z-Score Outlier Detection

    Computes mean and standard deviation of cost-per-work and duration-per-work across the national dataset. Works with z-score > 3.0 flagged as cost or duration anomalies. Per-member anomaly rate computed as anomalous / total works.

  • IQR (Interquartile Range) Method

    Used as a secondary check for skewed distributions. Values below Q1 − 1.5×IQR or above Q3 + 1.5×IQR flagged. Particularly effective for detecting extreme fund allocation outliers in small-sample MP/MLA groups.

  • Statistical Process Control (SPC)

    Control charts track monthly expenditure and completion rates against rolling 12-month baselines. Points beyond ±2σ trigger early-warning alerts in the AI Risk Centre before full anomaly classification.

Trend & Time-Series Analysis

  • Rolling Window Aggregation

    3-month and 6-month rolling averages smooth short-term volatility in fund flow and completion metrics. Enables meaningful quarter-over-quarter comparison without seasonal noise.

  • Linear Regression Trend Lines

    Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) fitted to monthly expenditure time series. Slope magnitude and direction classify each member as Accelerating, Stable, or Decelerating. R² reported for trend confidence.

  • Moving Average Convergence

    Short-term (3-month) vs long-term (6-month) moving average crossovers detect inflection points in fund disbursement velocity. Crossover events surface in the performance timeline visualizations.

Risk Scoring Model

  • Multi-Factor Risk Index

    Combines 4 risk signals into a single 0–100 risk score: anomaly_rate (35% weight), low_utilization flag (25%), zero_completion flag (25%), high_pipeline_ratio (15%). Each factor min-max normalized before weighted sum.

  • Severity Classification

    Risk index mapped to 4 severity levels: CRITICAL (≥75), HIGH (≥50), MODERATE (≥25), LOW (<25). Thresholds calibrated against historical intervention data to minimize false positives.

  • Confidence Intervals

    Every risk score includes a confidence metric based on sample size (number of works). Entities with <5 works receive lower confidence flags. Bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) used for small-sample confidence estimation.

Key Statistical Principles

Reproducibility: All computations are deterministic given the same DB1 snapshot. No random seeds, no stochastic elements in production scoring.
Explainability: Every score and classification can be traced back to its input indicators and weights. No black-box neural networks or ensemble methods.
Conservatism: When data is insufficient (<5 works), the system defaults to NO_DATA or LOW confidence rather than producing potentially misleading scores.

Data Pipeline Architecture

Govsense AI operates a two-database architecture with a change-triggered full rebuild pipeline. This ensures data integrity while enabling rapid analytical updates.

DB1 — Raw Operational Store

  • 1.Direct ingestion from MoSPI / NIC MPLADS portal
  • 2.Raw work records, member profiles, fund allocations
  • 3.Fingerprint-based change detection (SHA-256)
  • 4.Preserves original data integrity — no transformations

DB2 — Analytical Intelligence Store

  • 1.All derived tables, ML scores, and intelligence layer
  • 2.Performance scores, risk classifications, peer benchmarks
  • 3.AI-generated analysis narratives per entity
  • 4.Cluster labels, anomaly flags, trend indicators

Known Limitations & Disclaimers

  • •Data reflects publicly available MPLADS records from the NIC portal. Govsense AI does not have access to internal government systems.
  • •Performance scores are derived indicators, not official government rankings. They should be interpreted as analytical insights, not verdicts.
  • •Risk flags are probabilistic assessments based on statistical anomaly detection. They do not imply misconduct or wrongdoing.
  • •Data freshness depends on the NIC portal update cycle. There may be a lag between ground reality and available records.
  • •MLA data coverage varies by state. Some states may have incomplete records.
